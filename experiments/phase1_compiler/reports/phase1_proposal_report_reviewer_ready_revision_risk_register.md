# Phase 1 Proposal Report Reviewer-Ready Revision Risk Register

Status: M5 risk register, 2026-06-01.

This register supports `docs/research/phase-1-proposal-report-v2.md`. It treats
risks as proposal-governance inputs, not as reasons to overstate or abandon the
current evidence.

| Risk | Why it matters | Why it does not invalidate the proposal | Next-phase response | Claim still prohibited |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Failed weighted design | The old target-profile weighting produced large paid-pilot gaps and should not be defended as the mainline. | A failed design is diagnostic evidence that naive weighting is unsafe under sparse support; it does not show benchmark compilation is impossible. | Keep old weighting as a negative control, require support thresholds before any weighting revival, and compare against simple baselines first. | The old weighted design is the promoted compiler. |
| Random evidence versus simple-baseline edge | The current candidate beats/ties `93.4%` of 1000 same-budget random selections, but the edge over the best simple aggregate baseline is only `0.0059` MAE. | The random comparison shows selection signal; the small edge correctly limits the claim to optimization traction. | Optimize against M4's `0.02` MAE margin, mandatory baselines, and adapter/repo/window diagnostics. | The current candidate already satisfies future validation standards. |
| Current candidate not ready for paid validation | M4 classifies the candidate as `diagnostic_traction_candidate_not_paid_ready`. | This is a clear project-stage target, not a rejection of the proposal. The proposal asks for no-paid hardening work. | Repair fallback/support, strengthen no-paid diagnostics, freeze the validation package, then require a separate paid-readiness decision. | Current evidence authorizes paid ACUT validation. |
| Fallback and composite policy | `6/18` selected slots use fallback, and boltons is `6/6` fallback. | Labeled fallback makes the limitation auditable instead of hidden. | Repair feature support before future outcomes are joined, or narrow the claim to a composite selector with fallback sensitivity. | Uniform coverage-policy claim across all repos. |
| Adapter-specific support | Codex fails while Kilo passes under current diagnostics. | Adapter differences are part of the ACUT configuration estimand, so they can be reported directly. | Keep per-named-ACUT reporting primary and use pooled metrics only as preregistered secondary diagnostics. | Cross-adapter or model-only superiority wording from current evidence. |
| Task-generator scope drift | Public task generators and agent gyms could pull the project away from benchmark compilation. | The report makes source systems Layer 1 inputs and keeps release construction as the research object. | Treat external/generated tasks as untrusted source adapters until locally certified. | Barcarolle is a general SWE task factory or agent-training environment. |
| Source quality and release schema | Poor task statements, oracle leakage, licensing, and environment problems can break benchmark interpretation. | Phase 1 repaired click source context and M4 defines release fields for source, oracle, leakage, and artifact hygiene. | Enforce release-schema fields before inclusion and report rejected/repair-needed candidates. | Candidate supply is trusted without certification. |
| Post-hoc validation risk | Retrospective pseudo-future evidence can be influenced by already inspected outcomes or design choices. | M4 separates pseudo-future replay from true future or preregistered rolling-origin evidence. | Freeze repos, cutoffs, source supply, feature extraction, candidate policy, baselines, seeds, adapters, invalid rules, and gates before outcome joins. | Pseudo-future replay carries the north-star predictive-validity claim. |
| Budget and paid-validation boundary | The proposal could be mistaken as a spending request. | M5 is explicitly no-paid and leaves staffing, duration, owner categories, approval format, and paid budget ceiling to user decisions. | Keep paid validation blocked until no-paid gates and explicit user approval are both present. | A paid run is approved by the M5 report. |
| Source-supply support concentration | Current support is sparse and repo diagnostics are concentrated. | Sparse support is exactly why M4 added support thresholds and release-schema requirements. | Increase certified candidate pools, require at least `2x` selected budget per repo, and report insufficient support rather than forcing a claim. | Broad method claims from three sparse repos. |
| Public benchmark contamination and quality drift | Public benchmark material can be exposed in training data, and tests may reject correct solutions. | The proposal does not rely on public benchmark score rank as the validity claim; it uses public benchmarks as related work and source-supply context. | Keep source provenance, leakage checks, future holdouts, and frozen release artifacts in the validation package. | Public benchmark performance alone predicts target-repo future work. |
